02Publications

  1. Learning Without Looking: Image-Dependent Gains from Image-Free RLVR

    Haocun Ye et al.

    In preparation

    When RLVR lifts a vision-language model’s visual-reasoning scores, is the model learning to see — or just learning to take the test? Standard benchmarks can’t tell the two apart. A controlled train × test visual-visibility design separates the sources of the gain, and FlipTrack — paired images differing in one key visual fact, scored so the answer must flip — makes text-prior-only strategies score zero by construction. The recurring pattern: scores can rise while reliance on the image quietly degrades. Code and benchmark will be released with the paper.

  4. VersaFusion: A Versatile Diffusion-Based Framework for Fine-Grained Image Editing and Enhancement

    Haocun Ye, Xinlong Jiang, Chenlong Gao, Bingyu Wang, Wuliang Huang, Yiqiang Chen

    AAAI 2025

    A tuning-free dual-branch framework for fine-grained editing: a guidance branch reconstructs the source and stores its self-attention K/V, a generation branch edits under feature-correspondence guidance and masks, with proximal negative-prompt inversion in place of null-text optimization. One set of weights covers outpainting, resizing, style transfer, drag and face editing. AAAI 39(9), 9445–9453.

01 / 04

Learning Without Looking · FlipTrack

Is RLVR teaching the model to see, or to take the test? Training Qwen2.5-VL with GRPO under four visual conditions — real image, caption, text-only, gray placeholder — and testing each with and without images separates where the gains come from. FlipTrack, a counterfactual benchmark of paired images whose answers must flip, scores text-prior-only strategies at zero by construction. The pattern: benchmark scores can rise while the model’s reliance on the image quietly degrades.

02 / 04

CRAFT — The Diagnosis Is Already in the Notes

Does image–text fusion actually use the image? On 5,904 real visits across Aier Eye Hospital sites, a deliberately weak probe — frozen encoder, one linear head — still recovers the diagnosis from diagnosis-redacted records at 0.944 macro-AUROC; even a view keeping only which fields were filled in reaches 0.854, because which exams were ordered is itself a clue. CRAFT packages this as a portable audit: report each channel’s shortcut floor before claiming a fusion gain. I led the trustworthy-evaluation work; the code is open-source and is becoming a pre-release dataset check.

  • Shortcut learning

03 / 04

Ophthalmology LLM Training Pipeline

General-purpose LLMs answer ophthalmic questions vaguely, and public ophthalmic QA corpora are scarce — so this industry project built the corpus first: 610k items filtered from 11 open medical QA sets, 208k QA pairs generated from 212 OCR’d textbooks, paraphrase-augmented to 9.0M instructions. Then full-parameter SFT of Qwen2.5-14B on 2 × 8 A800s (DeepSpeed ZeRO-3, bf16, global batch 1024, RDMA / NCCL). A four-dimension LLM-judge score — medical accuracy, completeness, clinical usefulness, professionalism — rose from 2.6 to 4.1; the method was granted an invention patent. I was a core member and took the project through its final wrap-up.

04 / 04

VersaFusion: Tuning-Free Fine-Grained Image Editing

Text-to-image models generate well but edit poorly: text instructions miss their target, and drag-style methods either inherit GAN backbones or fine-tune per image. VersaFusion’s dual branches edit with no tuning at all — 5.23 s preparation versus 63.56 s for DragDiffusion, with the best point-alignment error and FID among general-purpose methods — and one set of weights covers outpainting, resizing, style transfer, drag and face editing. (AAAI 2025)

04About

Beyond the benchmarks

I’m Haocun Ye, a PhD student in artificial intelligence at the Institute of Computing Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, advised by Prof. Yiqiang Chen.

I work on multimodal large models — how they are trained, how they are post-trained with reinforcement learning, and how they should be evaluated. The question that drives most of my work is simple to state and hard to answer: when a benchmark score goes up, did the model actually acquire the ability the benchmark claims to measure?

Away from the lab I like experimenting in the kitchen, and I’m happiest on days off spent with family, travelling with friends, or around a board-game table — ideally with a dog nearby. In quieter hours I play single-player games (GTA, The Witcher 3, Sekiro). I know I have my share of flaws, and I try to keep improving — both in how I work with others and in myself.
